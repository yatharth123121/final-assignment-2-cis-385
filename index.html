Where code meets crops.

We ask for $100,000,000 to transform an underused agricultural growing area at UFV into an automated living laboratory: IoT sensors, machine-learning climate models and computer-vision plant monitoring, built and run with CIS and agriculture students.

The opportunity

Without a unified system, monitoring and adjusting growing conditions takes more manual effort, and it is harder to use data consistently for research and teaching. The living lab brings computer science and sustainable agriculture together and gives UFV students practical experience with connected systems, machine learning and controlled-environment agriculture.

Learning

Coursework, research projects and theses for CIS, agriculture and biology students.

Sustainability

Data-driven control aims to reduce energy and resource use. Benefits will be measured against a documented baseline.

Region

Fraser Valley growers, co-operatives and agritech partners get a local testbed.

Current-state challenges should be confirmed with UFV stakeholders before they are treated as established facts.

Four phases to launch

Method: hybrid. Waterfall for civil construction, retrofit and structural changes, where safety and zoning approvals come first. Agile Scrum in two-week sprints for software, the dashboard and AI model testing.

Technical architecture

A multi-bay glass greenhouse with automated thermal curtains, hydroponic troughs and vertical racks. Edge gateways collect data and send it by MQTT to a secure UFV cloud database.

Cost and change control

A $10 million contingency covers supply-chain inflation, equipment overruns and other approved risks. Scope changes and budget reallocations need a formal change request, approved by the sponsor and relevant UFV or grant authorities. Stage-gate reviews at the end of Phases 1 and 2 assess deliverables, risks, budget and readiness before the next phase or funding tranche is released.

Quality assurance plan

Phase 1: IoT

Acceptance test for every sensor and actuator, calibration records, and a baseline data-collection period so benefit claims can be checked against measured values.

Phase 2: ML and digital twin

Validate each model on held-out data, compare with the rule-based baseline, and record accuracy, water and energy use before automated control goes live.

Phase 3: computer vision

Evaluate detection on a labelled image set (precision and recall), with human review of flagged plants.

Milestone 7: beta testing and sign-off

User acceptance testing, security review of MQTT and dashboard layers, failure-mode tests (power, network, sensor loss) and a signed test report.

Compliance checks (to confirm with UFV)

Building, electrical and safety approvals before Milestone 2; UFV policy and BC privacy law for user accounts; grant reporting per tranche; food-safety rules if crops are sold in Phase 4.

Research and references

The project draws on controlled-environment agriculture work in British Columbia, including the B.C. Centre for Agritech Innovation at SFU and UFV’s BioPod infrastructure. Research suggests data-driven controlled-environment agriculture can help reduce water and energy use and improve yield consistency; the project will evaluate this against a documented baseline.
